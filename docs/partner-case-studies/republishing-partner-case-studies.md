# Republishing partner case studies

26 October 2026.

This process is for any partner. It is not written for one account.

## Purpose

Take case studies a partner has already published on their website and shared with us. Put them on Zoho's customers page, in Zoho's format.

- The customer's quote names the Zoho product and the outcome. We add the partner's name to that quote when it is required.
- The partner stays in the story, in Partner engagement.
- Customer marketing does not contact the customer. The partner does. Partner account managers stay on the thread with us.
- Nothing goes live without the partner's approval and the customer's permission on file, for Zoho's website.
- We rewrite. The partner reviews. The page is in the present tense.

What Zoho gets

- Stories on our customer pages about the product, with the partner in them.
- A customer quote tied to an outcome the customer actually stated: ROI, a process that runs end to end, pipeline visibility.
- Permission on file for the customer's name, the company name, and the products. A bundle is named as the products in it.
- One process, so a conversation with a partner is not a fresh negotiation.
- Later, new stories that arrive in our format, with consent already done, so publishing is not stuck on permission.

What the partner gets

- The customers page says who implemented it, in Partner engagement.
- Their quote is in the case study and in the partner LinkedIn post, once the page is live.
- Tier points already earned in Partner Store stay as they are. This page does not add points and does not remove them. An approved study is worth 10 points, and that stops at 5 studies.
- If the person quoted has left, the partner finds who can quote now and can refresh their own page at the same time. We do not refresh it for them.

## What the process includes

What we need from the partner, on each story

- The customer quote, judged by the test below.
- The customer's permission for Zoho's website.
- A review of the draft. That review is the partner's approval to publish.
- Every conversation with the customer about republishing the story.
- The partner's name, for the title.
- The partner's logo, for the side column.
- The address for a nofollow link to their site. The reader can visit. Zoho does not pass search credit. It is not a line telling the reader to hire the partner. The call to action at the end of the partner's own case study does not come onto our page. Partner engagement is the credit. The link is the visit.

The format

- Title. The customer, what changed, the product. The partner's name is not in the title today. We are adding it.
- Customer quote.
- The company.
- The challenge.
- The solution.
- Benefits and ROI.
- Partner engagement. How the partner came in, and the partner's quote.
- Side column. Industry, employees, type of business, the customer logo, and the partner logo.

The partner's name in the title, the partner logo, and the nofollow link are not on the page yet. We collect them with the story. We do not hold the story until the template can show them.

How the partner's text is rewritten

- Their title card becomes our title: the outcome and the product, with the partner's name added.
- Overview and executive summary become The company. A customer quote moves up only if it passes the test below.
- Their problem becomes The challenge.
- Evaluation, proposed solution, and implementation become The solution, written as what the product does now. How the partner did the work moves to Partner engagement.
- Their results become Benefits and ROI. A number comes across only if they say it is still true.
- Their quote, and how they were brought in, become Partner engagement.
- Their closing customer quote becomes the customer quote, if it passes. If it only praises the partner, we ask for another. We do not rewrite a failing sentence into a pass and put the customer's name under it.

The quote

A quote passes when a Zoho product is named and an outcome is named. The partner's name may be in it when that is required.

It fails when the partner is the only subject, or when the sentence is praise with no product and no outcome.

These lines were written for the test. They are not from a customer. Do not publish them.

Does not pass:

- "Our partner understood our business and changed how we work." No product. No outcome.
- "Zoho has been great." No product. No outcome.

Passes:

- "Zoho CRM is where the pipeline sits. Follow-ups go out without someone having to remember."
- "A ticket in Zoho Desk is on the same customer we already have in CRM."

The name on the quote has to be a person who will stand by it. If they have left, the old sentence does not get a new name. The partner gets a new sentence from someone who is there.

We do not invent an outcome to make a weak quote pass. That story waits, or it is held.

The rewrite tool

The tool takes the partner's published page, or the text submitted to Partner Store, and returns a draft in the sections above plus a flag list. A flag is not quietly edited away.

It flags a quote with no Zoho product, a quote with no outcome, a quote whose only subject is the partner, a quoted person we cannot confirm is still there, a date or a number that may be stale, a product outside CRM, Desk, and Campaigns, a bundle that does not name the products, a missing partner quote, and a story whose only partner credit is a call to action.

It does not add a number the source does not contain. A person reads every draft before it goes out. The tool does not email the partner and does not file permission. If it is not ready, we draft by hand.

The batch

1. Draft.
2. Send one batch. Five, six, or seven stories. Seven when the person quoted is still there and the facts are current. Five when we need new quotes. Each story goes with the checklist below.
3. The partner comes back with the quote, the customer's permission, and the corrections.
4. We revise once. If the second draft is still wrong, we stop and talk. We do not keep an open edit on the story.
5. A clear story is published. A story that is not clear is held, and it does not hold the others. Clear means the quote passes, the partner has approved the revision, and the customer's permission is on the sheet.
6. The next batch goes when the reviews on this one are back, not when every story is live. We can draft the next one while we wait. We do not send it early.

A story is held, and the reason is written on the sheet, when no one will quote, the customer will not give permission, the quote cannot pass unless we invent the outcome, the product is outside the three above, the draft is still wrong after one revision, or there is no proof of the form and no email. The partner is told which story, and why, in one line. A held story is not part of the next batch's count. It comes back when the reason is gone.

Checklist. One per story. The partner returns it.

- Title on their site, and the URL
- Person to quote: name, role, still at the company
- If that person has left: the new person's name and role
- The quote
- What in the old story is no longer true
- Products, if it was sold as a bundle
- Partner name for the title, logo file for the side column, and the site address for the nofollow link
- Consent: the form submitted, or the email attached
- Anything that must not be published

Consent

Permission for the partner's website is not permission for Zoho's website. Partner Store also asks the partner to have consent before submission and reuse: https://www.zoho.com/store/partner/help/partner-company-manage-case-studies.html

The Zoho form does not attach itself to the case study. Until it does, the link goes out with every batch: https://consent.zohocorp.com/#Consents

Gopal is checking the backend and will say how a submission gets tied to a story. Until then, "the partner says it was filled in" is not enough. The sheet needs something we can point at.

The customer's permission covers their name, the company name, the products in the story, and logo and images if we use any. The form already asks about logo and images. An email used instead has to say the same things.

The form is the first ask. If the customer will not fill it in, the partner sends the email below. The customer replies. The partner forwards that reply. We do not send the email. A phone call retold to us is not a file.

The words can be finished before permission arrives. The page cannot go live before it arrives.

One row per story, opened before the first draft goes out:

- Partner
- Their title, and their URL
- Our working title
- Customer name, as it will appear
- Company
- Products named in the consent
- Form or email
- Date
- Where the proof is
- Our URL, once the page is live
- Status: draft, with the partner, revising, cleared, published, held
- If held, the reason, in one line

When submissions start attaching on their own, this sheet is what gets attached. It is not thrown away first.

Email, if the customer refuses the form. The partner sends it.

Subject: Permission to publish your story on Zoho's website

We are working with Zoho on a version of the story that is already on our website. Their version is rewritten for their site. It needs your permission, separate from the permission you gave us.

Reply to this email with a yes or a no.

A yes covers:

- your name, [name]
- [company]
- [Zoho CRM, Zoho Desk, Zoho Campaigns — only the ones in the story]
- your logo, and any photo we already use, on that page

If you would rather use the form, it is here: https://consent.zohocorp.com/#Consents

We ask for the form first. This email is for when you do not want to fill the form in.

A new customer, with no story yet, is introduced to the marketer who already writes original stories with partners. That work stays as it is.

## Short term goals

- One partner at a time. Agree the set, then share it in the batches above.
- Products: CRM, Desk, and Campaigns.
- Bring the partner account managers in before the first batch, and keep them on the thread. They keep us connected to the partner. They do not talk to the customer in our place.
- Agree promotion with that account team before a page is live. After publish, the partner LinkedIn post uses Partner engagement and the partner quote. A story still in review is not posted. Customer marketing rewrites, sends the batch, and publishes. The account team and the partner handle the customer.
- Customer permission follows the consent steps above, including the form link with each batch, until a submission attaches to the story on its own.
- The first partner is also how we check the rewrite tool. A person still edits and sends. We do not wait for the tool to start.
- No date on the wider list until that first batch shows how long a quote and a permission take.

## Long term goals

- The same process for the stories on Find a Partner. The count in hand is 1,327. A diagnostic follows. Do not plan on any other number.
- The same three products, unless we add one later. Stories held for being outside those products are picked up first if we do.
- A story that is in a partner's set and also on Find a Partner is done once.
- Once a partner's existing stories are republished, the next story is written in this format and the customer fills the consent form before we draft. Whether every partner hears that after the full list, or each partner hears it when their own stories are done, is still open. Waiting for the last story leaves new stories, signed during the backlog, on today's permission problem.
- The partner-led CRM webinars, September to December 2026, run alongside this. A published story with permission on the sheet can be the use case a partner presents. We do not hold a case study for a webinar date, and we do not approach that customer. Republication keeps supplying the sessions. The sessions keep giving partners a place to show the work.
